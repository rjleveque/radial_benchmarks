# Designing a test problem with axial symmetry

The goal is to design test problems for tsunami benchmarking based on:

- A 1D topography profile
- A 1D seafloor deformation
- Fine resolution 1D simulations to compute a reference solution
- 2D topography and seafloor deformations generated by rotating the 1D
  profiles about a specified axis on the sphere.

The 2D topography and seafloor deformation can be captured on 2D
longitude-latitude grids at realistic resolution to simulate the data that
might be provided for solving a real problem.

Note that rotating the 1D topography gives a shoreline that lies around a
circle on the surface of the sphere.
The 2D solution along any transect that is a
great circle starting at a pole defined by the
axis of revolution and going through a point on
this shoreline should agree with the 1D solution computed with the same
model equations (e.g. nonlinear shallow water equations) provided that the
1D equations include a source term for the axial symmetry. (And assuming the
2D code is solving in spherical longitude-latitude coordinates.)


## Sample 1D topography

Any 1D topography specifying ground elevation (relative to MHW) as a
function of distance can be used.  For this test, realistic topography was
obtained by sampling a great circle transect of
real topography from the deep ocean to a coastal location just south of
Westport, WA.  The best choice of topography is open to discussion.

The offshore topography was sampled from the etopo 2022 15-arcsecond (15") data,
while the nearshore and onshore topography was sampled from the NOAA CUDEM
1/9" data. This data was extended by constant values offshore and
onshore to obtain 1D data defined from 0 roughly 2600 km in 1D, with the
shoreline at 2518.589 km, shown below:

We can define an "axial latitude" based on degrees from the equator relative
to an axis of revolution through the sphere.  This would simply be the 
latitude if the axis of revolution is chosen to go through the earth's
poles, but we choose some other axis so that the 2D solution on the
resulting domain does not simply reduce to a being constant in longitude.  

One degree of latitude is $\approx 111$ km, so in axial latitude
coordinates the shoreline is approximately 22 degrees south of the axial
pole (the point where the axis of symmetry passes through the earth).
Solving the 1D equations with axial symmetry is accomplished by adding a
source term involving the axial latitude. The spherical source term is
described, for example, in Section 5 of [these
notes](https://faculty.washington.edu/rjl/misc/spherical_swe_2023-10-27.pdf).

### 1D earthquake deformation

The seafloor deformation generated by an earthquake can likewise be
specified as an arbitrary 1D function of distance.  We could choose
a transect of an actual earthquake deformation, but for this test a
synthetic source is specified based on a positive Gaussian centered
roughly at the trench and a broader and smaller amplitude  negative
Gaussian closer to shore that results in some coastal subsidence.

The function used was based on examining transects of other sources such as
the L1 source along the same transect used to define the topography.
For the initial test the amplitude was set to be
only about half the amplitude of L1 to give less inundation.  Parameters
can easily be adjusted to change the amplitude, Gaussian widths, amount of
coastal subsidence, etc.

### 1D convergence

Before introducing the 2D problem, it is important to insure that the
1D solution looks reasonable and appears to converge as the 1D grid is
refined.

For these tests a nonuniform grid was used in 1D with finer resolution
onshore and transitioning smoothly to coarser resolution in the deep ocean.
Since in realistic nearfield tsunami modeling problems we often use 1/3"
grids onshore (with roughly 10 m resolution) and 15" grids in the source
region (with roughly 500 m resolution) we choose this as one resolution to
test in 2D.  We then refined this grid everywhere by factors of 2, 5, and 10
to get finer grids with onshore resolutions of 5, 2, and 1 meter, as well as
coarsening by a factor of 3 for an onshore resolution of 30 m (1").

## Axis of revolution and 2D domain

For this test, the axis of revolution was chosen to pass through the earth at
longitude 50, latitude 40 (the axial pole).  
The plot below shows the resulting "ocean" when plotted in standard
longitude-latitude coordinates on the earth (it would look circular if
plotted on the sphere).  This plot also shows several great circle transects
in red that go from the axial pole to various points along the shore.
A transect of the 2D solution along any of these curves should agree with
the 1D solution.

rotated 1D topography at different resolutions:

- 15" resolution covering the full domain,
- 3" covering the continental shelf and shore near the coastal location,
- 1/3" covering the nearshore and onshore at the coastal location.
